02 — Resources
The Four Cost Drivers
What it actually takes to train a frontier language model
🗄️
Resource 01
Datasets

LLMs require massive, diverse text corpora. Sources include CommonCrawl, Wikipedia, Books3, GitHub, arXiv, and curated human-written content. Data quality directly impacts capability.

GPT-4: ~13T tokens · LLaMA 3: 15T tokens
💻
Resource 02
Computational Power

Training requires thousands of high-end GPUs or TPUs running in parallel for months. NVIDIA A100/H100 GPUs are standard. Interconnect bandwidth between chips is critical.

GPT-4: ~25,000 A100s · Gemini: TPU v4 pods
⚡
Resource 03
Energy Consumption

Training large models consumes enormous electricity, raising serious environmental concerns. Inference at scale also adds significant ongoing energy costs across millions of daily queries.

GPT-3: ~1,287 MWh · GPT-4: est. 50× more
⏱️
Resource 04
Time Required

Even with thousands of GPUs, pre-training takes weeks to months. Fine-tuning and RLHF add additional time. Safety testing, red-teaming, and evaluation extend timelines further.

GPT-3: ~34 days · Frontier models: 3–6+ months

Key Takeaways

Training large language models is one of the most resource-intensive endeavors in modern computing. Understanding the pipeline and costs reveals both the immense capability and responsibility behind these systems.

📈

Costs Are Skyrocketing

From $4.6M for GPT-3 in 2020 to $100M+ for frontier models today — costs roughly double every year.

🌍

Energy Is a Real Concern

Training one frontier model can emit as much CO₂ as hundreds of transatlantic flights.

🤝

Alignment Takes Time

RLHF and Constitutional AI add months to development but are essential for safe, helpful models.

🔓

Open vs. Closed Models

Meta's open-weights LLaMA democratizes access. OpenAI and Anthropic keep weights closed for safety.

📊

Data Quality Matters Most

More compute helps, but clean, diverse, high-quality training data has the greatest impact on capability.

🚀

Deployment Costs Add Up

Inference at scale (billions of queries) often exceeds the original training cost over the product's lifetime.
